Operator Kit Fluency · Lesson 01

The tiebreaker lives in the frontmatter

Your kit's real failure mode isn't forgetting a skill exists — it's two skills that sound alike. There is already a tiebreaker written into 58 of them. This lesson makes you fast at reading it.

Three layers, narrowing

Routing runs through three layers, each narrower than the last:

  1. CLAUDE.md — the routing table. Task phrase → skill. The only index Claude has for the 104 user-invoked skills.
  2. The pack's NAVIGATOR.md — a decision tree within one pack.
  3. The skill's own description: frontmatter — where the DO NOT use for… clause settles ties.

Layer 1 gets you to a neighbourhood. Layer 3 picks the house. Most routing mistakes happen because people stop at layer 1.

Why this is the highest-leverage lesson. CLAUDE.md diagnoses the problem itself: "the more near-identical descriptions compete, the more often the wrong one fires." At 141 skills you have a lot of competition. The DO NOT clause is the kit's built-in answer, and it's sitting in 58 skills unread.

Five scenarios

Pick without looking anything up. The explanation quotes the skill's own frontmatter.

1. Strategic angles

A competitor ships something that reframes the category. You want to think it through from several strategic angles — what it signals, what it forecloses, how you'd respond.

product-riff. The tiebreaker is written into the skill you'd probably reach for first: competitive-teardown says "DO NOT use for strategic product analysis from multiple angles — use pm-agents/product-riff instead." Teardown is for scoring, feature matrices and pricing. Riff is for product sense. domain-tools/competitive-teardown/SKILL.md, frontmatter

2. Next quarter's channels

You're planning paid media, SEO and partnership programs for next quarter, and you need the attribution model to go with them.

marketing-demand-acquisition — but read the caveat, it matters more than the answer.

The declared pair is clean: marketing-strategy-pmm says "DO NOT use for demand gen execution — use marketing-demand-acquisition instead." Positioning goes to pmm; running the channels goes here. That much the frontmatter settles.

The undeclared pair is not clean. acquisition-channel-advisor says it's for "deciding how to allocate marketing budget across channels." That overlaps this scenario directly, and neither skill mentions the other. The honest discriminator is emphasis — planning and running programs (demand-acquisition) vs a scale/test/kill verdict on channels (advisor) — but you had to infer it, because nobody wrote it down. Second prune signal of the lesson. domain-tools/marketing-strategy-pmm · pm-frameworks/acquisition-channel-advisor, frontmatter

3. The measurement plan

You need a full hierarchy: the outcome metric, the intermediate metrics beneath it, and the leading indicators that move first.

metrics-design. analytical-thinking says "DO NOT use for detailed metrics hierarchies or measurement plans — use metrics-design instead." Analytical-thinking is for North Star framing and tradeoffs; metrics-design builds the actual structure.

Worth knowing: metrics-design was missing from the routing table entirely until this week. Claude could not reach it at all. It was one of six in that state. pm-agents/skills/analytical-thinking/SKILL.md · see learning-records/0001

4. Two story maps

You already know the user workflow and the priorities. You just need the map itself as an artifact.

user-story-mapping. No DO NOT clause needed — the two descriptions split cleanly on where the knowledge comes from. This one is "from existing knowledge about user workflows and priorities." The -workshop variant is "a facilitated, interactive session to discover and build a story map from scratch."

That split repeats across your kit: customer-journey-map vs -mapping-workshop is the same pair. Artifact-from-what-you-know vs. session-to-discover — learn it once, it routes four skills. pm-frameworks/user-story-mapping{,-workshop}/SKILL.md

5. The battlecard

Sales needs a card for handling "why not Competitor X?" on live calls.

There is no right answer, and that is the lesson. Both produce a battlecard. Neither carries a DO NOT clause pointing at the other. competitive-battlecard is "sales-ready competitive battlecards… positioning, feature comparison, objection handling"; competitive-intelligence is "research competitors and build an interactive battlecard."

When two skills overlap and neither declares the boundary, routing is a coin flip — and per CLAUDE.md's own reasoning, competing descriptions make the wrong one fire. That is a prune signal, not a memorisation problem. One should absorb the other, or one should declare the boundary. gtm-tools/competitive-battlecard · sales-tools/skills/competitive-intelligence

The win

When two skills sound alike, open the frontmatter and look for DO NOT use for… — 58 skills carry one, and it names the correct alternative outright. When there isn't one and the overlap is real, you haven't found a hard routing problem; you've found two skills that should be one.
